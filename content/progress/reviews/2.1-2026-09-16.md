### The middle-duplicate defect, and a gap in the fix that closes it

**The middle-duplicate defect is genuine and correctly reasoned, with one caveat.** I traced it independently. `{"tenant":"tA","body":"x","tenant":"tC","tenant":"tA"}` gives the vulnerable reader `acl = "tA"` (regex first match) and `stored = "tA"` (CPython last-wins), so a first-versus-last check finds agreement and accepts. That is real, and `04-build.md`'s Candidate A / Candidate B comparison is honest work: it names the fix a competent engineer would actually write first and shows the input that defeats it. The caveat is that `03-break.md` L40's framing — that a `"tC"` claim was "silently overwritten by nobody's decision" — is weaker than it sounds, because no standard duplicate-key policy (first-wins, last-wins, error, collect-all) would yield `"tC"` *alone*. The defensible reading, which the module does reach at `04-build.md` L32, is that any duplicate key makes the object ambiguous and first-versus-last is merely a proxy that happens to be exact for two keys. That is correct; the "a third reader would have chosen tC" implication is not established.

**The fix that closes it has its own gap, of exactly the same class.** `fixed/parse_note.py` L29 collects occurrences with a raw-text regex, `TENANT_RE = re.compile(r'"tenant"\s*:\s*"([^"]*)"')`, and L41 requires `len(set(occurrences)) == 1 and acl == stored`. Because that regex matches *bytes* rather than *members*, any duplicate `tenant` member written with a JSON escape is invisible to it while `json.loads` still collapses it. Verified against the real file at `685fb19`:

| Input | `json.loads` sees | `fixed/` returns | Should be |
|---|---|---|---|
| `{"tenant":"tB","tenant":"tA"}` | one `tenant`, last-wins `tA` | **accepted=True**, acl=stored=`tA` | refused — two disagreeing claims (`tB`, `tA`) |

| `{"tenant":1,"tenant":"tA"}` | `tA` | **accepted=True** | refused — a numeric claim and a string claim disagree |

**`quality: developing`**, and **`depth` stands at `map-complete`.** Two critical dimensions score 1 — Assessment alignment and Standards accuracy — and the rubric's decision rules are explicit that a critical dimension below 2 blocks `depth: publishable` and cannot be averaged away. It would be easy to average it away here, because the average is good: four dimensions score 3, the prose derives rather than asserts, the word floor carried reasoning rather than padding, the lab fails for the security reason, and the anti-fake pair survived the strongest fake I could build. This module is closer to publishable than its verdict sounds.

What blocks it is not prose quality but truthfulness about three things a learner would act on. The module tells learners that ASVS V1.5.3 is an allow-list validation requirement when it is, verbatim, the requirement that different parsers of the same data type must parse consistently — the module's own property, forfeited in the citation that should have carried it (Blocker 1). And it ships a fix whose gap is the one its own two lessons name as disqualifying for text-matching checks, while asserting that the fix "subsumes" the alternative and checks "all of them" (Blocker 3). Any one of those would be a blocker; together they mean the module's central claims about a standard, a transfer mechanism and its own artifact are each wrong in a way a careful learner could discover and a careless one would carry away.

Blockers 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 are module-level bookkeeping of exactly the kind 1.4 was held on, and they close with edits to `spec.md`, `rubric.md`, `README.md`, `03-break.md`, `08-review.md`, `keys/2.1.md` and `pins.yaml`.
